What the simulator computes

One transfer through a single air-displacement channel with a conical 200 µL tip: aspirate from a reservoir, move 1 s, free-dispense into a well, blow out, lift. The liquid column is a lumped plug pushed by an isothermal air spring. Each run takes a few milliseconds, so the optimizer can try hundreds of liquid classes in a second or two.

Column dynamics

ρ·I(h)·dQ/dt = P_out − P_air − ρ·g·h + 2σ·cosθ / r(h) − [ R(h) + ½ρ|Q| / A₀² ]·Q P_air = n / V_air isothermal air gap (150 µL channel + tip) R(h) = 8μ/(3πk)·(r₀⁻³ − r(h)⁻³) + 3μ/r₀³ Poiseuille in the cone + Sampson orifice term I(h) = (1/πk)·(1/r₀ − 1/r(h)) + 2/(πr₀) inertance of the column

The piston stroke includes a static correction for the column weight, like the correction curve in a real liquid class. What is left is dynamic error, which is what speeds, delays and blowout control.

Limits

Coefficients come from textbook scaling laws and typical polypropylene contact angles, not from a calibrated instrument. Use the results for intuition and as a cheap prior for real gravimetric tuning, not as settings to copy to a robot. Volatile liquids, foaming, non-Newtonian samples and contact dispense are out of scope.
